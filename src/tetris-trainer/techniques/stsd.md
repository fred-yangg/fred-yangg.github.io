# STSD

Status: draft, not reviewed.
Sources: [four.lol](https://four.lol/methods/stsd), [Hard Drop wiki](https://harddrop.com/wiki/STSD).

## What it is

Super T-Spin Double: one setup, two TSDs. The first T drops into a deep
notch, then turns into it with the TST kick (the kick that moves a T down two
rows), clearing two lines. The second T then does an ordinary TSD in the slot
the first one leaves.

```
Look for this     Create notch      Create overhang   First TSD         Second TSD
..........        ..........        ..ZZ......        ..ZZ......        ..ZZ......
..........        .....#####        ...ZZ#####        ...ZZ#####        TTTZZ#####
.....#####        JJ.#######        JJ.#######        JJT#######        JTt#######
...#######        J..#######        J..#######        JTT#######
...#######        J..#######        J..#######        J.T#######
...#######
```

`t` is the first T's leftover cell. The thing to recognize is a 3-wide, 3-tall
empty space next to the stack. A J or L in it makes the notch, and another
piece on top makes the overhang. The first T ends up standing sideways, deep in
the notch.

## When to use it

- The rows are too high for a normal TSD, or you want to fit a second TSD in.
- Over a Tetris column. Hard Drop calls this "very deadly" with Tetrises: as
  long as the notch isn't set up yet, you can still take the Tetris.
- To recover from a misdropped J or L.
- During a T drought, an STSD can funnel other setups (for example, one on an
  Imperial Cross).

When not to use it:

- A garbage hole in the wrong column (the third column from the edge in the
  usual pictures) breaks it. A Boomerang (a J or L spun in) can save that
  board.
- With S/Z as the overhang, you need another piece on top too, so check the
  queue more carefully.

## Starting points

1. **A 3×3 empty space beside the stack.** Place J (or L) to make the notch,
   then the overhang.
2. **Over a Tetris column.** Build the STSD above the open column you were
   saving.
3. **On top of a TSD (Trinity).** An STSD stacked on a TSD. Often converted
   from a plain TSD when no T is coming soon.
4. **From DT or BT Cannon shapes (DT STSD, BT STSD).** The first TSD forms an
   STSD, for three TSDs in total.

```
Over a Tetris column (the open column under the L)
......SS..
.....SS...
#######.LL
#######..L
#######..L
########.#
########.#
########.#
```

## Pieces

- J or L for the notch. The J+L pair is the most common because you only need
  to see both in the queue.
- The overhang: J, L, I, O, S or Z. S/Z needs one more piece on top.
- Two Ts. Hard Drop suggests holding a T until you can do both TSDs.

## What it leaves

Four rows cleared. The overhang piece's leftovers stay on top. The second TSD
can instead become a TST or a double TST if you can wait a bag (works best
with an S overhang and the right side already stacked).

## How the trainer should test it

- **Start:** a stack with a 3×3 gap beside it, or a Tetris column with room
  above it, or a TSD to convert. The preview has J/L and an overhang piece,
  with Ts coming.
- **Goal:** the first TSD with the T sideways in the notch, then the second
  TSD, finishing clean.
- **Wrong-call positions:** a garbage hole in the wrong column (a Boomerang
  instead), no second T in reach, or a Tetris being the better play.
- **Built so far:** a 3×3 space beside the stack, sometimes over a Tetris
  column, with the J (or L) and an overhang piece in the preview, and
  sometimes a spare. No wrong calls: with the J swapped out, no other pair of
  TSDs fits. Not yet: Trinity, cannon shapes, and the Boomerang position.
