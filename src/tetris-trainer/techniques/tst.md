# TST setups

Status: draft, not reviewed.
Source: [four.lol](https://four.lol/methods/tspin-triple).

## What it is

A T-Spin Triple: the T goes in standing sideways, clearing three rows. The
slot is a 1-wide column with a side pocket under an overhang, and the T gets
in with the TST kick.

```
Slot              TST
####......        ####......
###.......        ###.......
###.######        ###T######
###..#####        ###TT#####
###.######        ###T######
```

Four.lol names four main ways to build one mid-game.

## Starting points

1. **Notch.** A one-cell notch at the bottom of a 3-wide gap. The most
   recognizable shape. L+J is one way to fill it, and other pieces can make a
   cleaner stack. An STMB Cave also works from a notch.

   ```
   Notch             Built with L and J
   ...#######        ..LL......
   ...#######        ...L......
   .#.#######        JJ.L######
                     J..#######
                     J#.#######
   ```

2. **Two-step stair.** The stack drops two steps into a 1-wide well. Compare
   with how you'd build a Fin TSD or Neo TSD from the same shape. S and Z can
   build the stair, which is useful for TST donations.

   ```
   Two-step stair    O as the overhang
   ........##        .......OO.
   ........##        .......OO.
   ######..##        ........##
   #######.##        ........##
                     ######..##
                     #######.##
   ```

3. **One-step stair with S or Z.** With only one step, an S or Z is the easiest
   overhang.
4. **From a TSD (TSD conversion).** Incomplete TSDs, TSDs and Neo TSDs can be
   turned into TSTs. Useful in Triple Doubles to fix a bad overhang, or to turn
   a TSD donation into a TST donation.

## When to use it

- Bigger attack than a TSD from the same column, when the stack has the notch
  or stairs already.
- TSD → TST: an S/Z overhang that leaves a cell unfilled lets you follow up
  with another TST or a TSD.
- Double TST as a spike. It's risky mid-game, so it's usually built early,
  before there's garbage.

When not to use it:

- Sometimes a TSD is better. Four.lol's example is a TST that would leave an
  unclean field.
- A TST always leaves an overhang to clean up (below). If you can't clean it
  up well, a TSD may be the better trade.

## What it leaves

Always an overhang. Ways to clean it up:

- **J or L slid under it** (the traditional way, keeping Back-to-Back). You can
  plan for this by matching the other side and building a TSD overhang ahead
  of time.
- **A Kaidan,** when the TST's overhang was an S or Z.
- **Shachiku Train,** when the S/Z overhang was placed flat.
- **A T-Spin Mini** works, but four.lol suggests saving the T for a Tetris or
  TSD instead.

## Related

- **Incomplete TSTs:** cleanup is usually a J/L slid underneath, sometimes
  spun in.
- **Double TST:** from a J+Z or L+S platform with the right side built, from a
  TSD with a platform for an S/Z, or converted from an STSD.
- **Infinite TST:** a looping TST pattern for Ultra, often after a DT Cannon.
- **King Crimson:** a TST on an STSD. Rarely used.

## How the trainer should test it

- **Start:** a realistic stack with a notch, a two-step stair, a one-step stair,
  or a TSD to convert. Random preview with the overhang pieces and a T.
- **Goal:** a TST in that slot, finishing on the cleanest board.
- **Follow-up puzzles:** start right after a TST and clean up the overhang
  (J/L slide, Kaidan, Shachiku Train).
- **Wrong-call positions:** where a TSD is the better choice, and where the
  TST's overhang can't be cleaned up well.
- **Built so far:** the notch and the two-step stair, with the pieces that
  finish the slot from there. Not yet: the one-step stair with S/Z, TSD
  conversion, cleanup puzzles after a TST, and wrong calls.
